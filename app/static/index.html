CiproTyper

A bioinformatics database for browsing, analyzing, and predicting
enterobacterial resistance to ciprofloxacin antibiotic

Currently hosting 2,734 genomes of Escherichia coli, Shigella sonnei, Salmonella enterica subspecies I, and Klebsiella pneumoniae isolates (1,669 resistant, 1,065 susceptible to ciprofloxacin), represented by 283 clonal groups (as detected by multilocus sequence typing) and 64 ciprotype combinations across 28 countries.

Five modules

PredictUpload or paste a genome sequence, or enter a BV-BRC ID or the residues, and get the ciprotype, the sequence type (ST clonal group), and the predicted phenotype. Download results as PDF, CSV, and JSON. BrowseFilter the repository by species, isolation source, ST, ciprotype, country, and year. Compare lab, BV-BRC, and CiproTyper calls. AnalyzeSee which ciprotype occurs in which isolation source, clonal group (ST), or country. SubmitContribute an isolate and track its status with your submission ID. HelpExplore the grammar behind the call, documentation, the API, references, and information about the terms and privacy policy through the Help menu and About page.

Theory

Ciprofloxacin binds to GyrA and ParC through a water–magnesium bridge involving a serine and an acidic residue. The effect of a change at one binding position depends on the residues around it.

Reading a ciprotype

Read the seven entries from left to right: GyrA-83, GyrA-87, ParC-80, ParC-84, RecN-346, qnrB2, qnrS1. For example, in L-N-I-E-L-0-0, the first four letters form the tetratype, the fifth gives the RecN residue, and the two zeros indicate that both qnr markers were reported absent. A 1 indicates presence.

How CiproTyper chooses a prediction

Tier 1. For a known ciprotype, the reference-majority call uses matching isolates from the selected species and source. If that source has no match, CiproTyper pools matches across sources for the same species. A warning appears when fewer than five reference isolates match or the majority share is below 80%.

Tier 2. For a ciprotype unseen in that species, the three checkpoints below determine the call. Tier 1 reports also show the checkpoint result for comparison.

    The three checkpoints

    1. GyrA and ParC. Read the four residues together. Some residues, such as D and E, appear in both columns. All four must fit the same column for that column’s call to apply. If neither column fits, the checkpoint is inconclusive.

    ParC exception. When serine (S) at ParC-80 occurs together with lysine (K) at ParC-84, the pair counts toward the Resistant column. Checkpoint 1 gives a Resistant call if both GyrA residues also fit that column.

    2. RecN-346. Leucine encoded by CTA gives Susceptible. Leucine encoded by CTG, or phenylalanine, gives Resistant. Other or unknown states are inconclusive.

    3. qnr markers. Either marker present gives Resistant. Both absent gives Susceptible.

    If any checkpoint gives a Resistant result, the combined call is Resistant. Inconclusive checkpoints are skipped. If none of the evaluated checkpoints gives a Resistant result, the combined call is Susceptible. Helix hydrophobicity is shown for comparison only.

    Sequence types and MLST

    Multilocus sequence typing (MLST) assigns a sequence type (ST) when all seven alleles match exactly and their combination has a registered profile. The table lists the Achtman schemes for E. coli, S. sonnei and S. enterica, and the Diancourt scheme for K. pneumoniae.

    SpeciesSeven housekeeping genes
    E. coli and S. sonneiadk, fumC, gyrB, icd, mdh, purA, recA
    S. enterica subsp. IaroC, dnaN, hemD, hisD, purE, sucA, thrA
    K. pneumoniaegapA, infB, mdh, pgi, phoE, rpoB, tonB

    Up to four representative alleles per locus help locate the gene in the assembly. CiproTyper looks up the extracted sequence and searches the full allele set for a located gene if the lookup fails. A tilde (~) marks a nearest-allele match. CiproTyper reads local allele and profile files distributed through Torsten Seemann’s open-source mlst repository.

    Why an ST may be unassigned

    For ST assignment, upload or paste a whole-genome assembly that includes all seven MLST genes. If a gene-only file or pasted sequence lacks any of these genes, or you enter only amino acid letters, CiproTyper can still predict a phenotype when enough markers are available, but it cannot assign an ST.

    First check for missing or fragmented genes, ambiguous bases and typing errors. If all seven loci are readable, the profile may already have an ST assigned after 31 December 2024. Changes to PubMLST’s redistribution terms limit the PubMLST-derived data bundled by Torsten Seemann’s mlst project to records available by that date. Another possibility is a new allele or combination. Check the current scheme before reporting a new ST. See the scheme references.

    How to read a sequence warning

    Chromosomal genes. A match to gyrA, parC or recN counts as detected from 75% nucleotide identity. If a detected gene matches the selected species reference at less than 90% identity (the default cutoff), CiproTyper displays “Please verify the species selected and resubmit” and withholds the ST, phenotype and checkpoints. Coverage below 90% of the reference gene also raises a warning.

    MLST matches. For inputs of at least 1,000,000 bp, a mismatch warning appears if any of the seven MLST loci in the selected species’ scheme is missing or matches at less than 90% nucleotide identity. These matches help check whether the assembly is consistent with the selected species. If MLST fails because of a processing error, CiproTyper may still report a phenotype without assigning an ST.

    Partial sequences. A missing chromosomal gene can leave Checkpoint 1 inconclusive. A readable RecN-346 or a detected qnr marker may still allow a limited prediction from Checkpoints 2 and 3 if the input passes the reference checks. If RecN-346 is unreadable and neither qnr marker is present, this partial route gives no phenotype. The qnr screen requires at least 80% identity and 90% reference coverage.

    Limits

    • Four species only: E. coli, S. sonnei, S. enterica subsp. I and K. pneumoniae. Choose the species yourself. A genome that does not match it gets no result. E. coli and S. sonnei share the same seven-locus MLST scheme. Their marker sequences can be similar enough for a genome to match either reference, so these matches alone cannot reliably distinguish the two species.
    • The founding dataset is limited. It holds 2,734 isolates from 28 countries, more than half from the USA, and from only three isolation sources (stool, urine and respiratory). More populations need to be studied.
    • A qnr gene found in the sequence does not prove it is expressed. Some qnr-positive isolates in the data are susceptible.
    • Assembled sequence only. Raw sequencing reads are not supported yet. Contig breaks and ambiguous target codons can prevent a complete ciprotype or ST.
    • The qnr screen uses qnrB2 and qnrS1 references; it does not cover every qnr family or every resistance mechanism. Predictions do not replace laboratory susceptibility testing or provide an MIC.
    • ST assignment is limited by the bundled allele and profile data. Newer PubMLST entries or genuinely new profiles may be absent, as explained above. No ST assigned is not proof of a new ST.

    About Ciprotyper
    
  
    
    
    Ciprotyper is an open web server, reference repository and REST API for predicting ciprofloxacin resistance in Escherichia coli, Shigella sonnei, Salmonella enterica subsp. I and Klebsiella pneumoniae. It reads five codon positions in gyrA, parC and recN and the plasmid genes qnrB2 and qnrS1, joins them into a ciprotype, and explains every call with the reference isolates and rules behind it. The Help menu has the rules, the documentation and the API.

    Privacy policy
    Last updated November 2026. ← About
    
  
    
    
    What we do not collect. There are no accounts, no advertising and no tracking or analytics. The site sets no cookies and stores nothing in your browser. Sequences you upload for a prediction are deleted after analysis.
    
    
    Predictions. A genome you upload, paste or fetch with a BV-BRC ID is analysed in a temporary folder and deleted when the request ends. The prediction is not stored. For a BV-BRC ID, the ID is sent to BV-BRC to get the assembly. The result of an identical request may be reused for a short time.
    
    
    Submissions. When you submit an isolate we store the ciprotype, the residues and the qnr result, the sequence type, the prediction and the details you enter: species, isolation source, genome ID and name, country, year, laboratory phenotype, your name, affiliation, publication link and contact email, the time of your agreement and the version of the consent notice. Each submission gets a submission ID. The genome file is not stored.
    
    
    Who sees what.
    
    
      
      
    • If an isolate is approved, it is public: the isolate data, your submitter name, affiliation, publication link and the submission date are shown in Browse and in the downloads and API.
      • 
      
    • Your email stays private. Only the curators use it, to contact you about your submission.
      • 
      
    • Anyone who has your submission ID can see its status and the prediction, but not your name or email.
      • 
    
    
    
    Stored on the server. Submission records, including the email address, are stored on the server and are available only to the maintainers. When you enter a BV-BRC genome ID, the ID is sent to BV-BRC to get the assembly; nothing else about you is sent.
    
    
    IP addresses and logs. Your IP address is used only to limit repeated requests and is not stored with your submission. The hosting service may keep its own logs.
    
    
    Keeping and removing data. Submissions are kept until they are removed. To correct or remove your submission, write to jisiasr.sclab@gmail.com with your submission ID. We can remove the record and take an approved isolate out of the repository, the statistics and the predictions. Copies that other people already downloaded cannot be recalled.
    
    
    Changes. We may update this policy and will change the date above.
